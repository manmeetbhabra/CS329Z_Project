## 1 Introduction

This Emergency Response Plan (ERP) has been prepared by Lithium Nevada Corporation (LNC) for the Thacker Pass Project (Project), located in northern Humboldt County, Nevada. This ERP outlines the various systems LNC will implement for emergency preparedness at the Project in accordance with 29 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) 1910.38(a).

The current mine life schedule results in 41 years of commercial mining production with two years of pre-production waste rock removal and stripping concurrent with process facility construction and at least five years of reclamation activities at the end of mine life.

## 2 Emergency Response Plan Objectives

This ERP is a requirement of Federal, State and Local Authorities and outlines the response procedures and preventive measures that are essential for effective and timely management of an emergency.

The ERP was developed and implemented to prepare for potential emergencies and to reduce the impact of any significant adverse event. The ERP is used to respond to and manage emergency situations and will be administered by LNC’s Safety and Environmental Department.

The objectives of the ERP are to:

- Provide for an effective response to emergency situations;

- Minimize the effect on personnel and surrounding communities;

- Minimize property and equipment losses;

- Coordinate interdepartmental responses;

- Assure the cooperation of outside agencies; and,

- Provide for the release of accurate information to the public.

## 3 Emergency Services and Contact Information

Depending on the nature of the emergency, mine personnel responding will first contact emergency services via two-way radios installed in vehicles and heavy equipment or hand-held units. Once the immediate threat has been stabilized, the Humboldt County Sheriff’s Office and additional regulatory agencies (as required) will be contacted.

<!-- page 6 | printed 2 -->

The Project site is not located in proximity to any urban areas, so the first responder to any emergency is likely to be on-site personnel. The closest dispatch center is provided by Humboldt General Hospital (HGH), located in Winnemucca, approximately 62 miles from the site. HGH is also the closest major medical center to the Project. This facility has an emergency room and other facilities adequate to handle emergencies that may occur. If immediate care is necessary, HGH has a helicopter stationed on site to provide rapid air transportation of critically injured/ill persons.

Emergency response vehicles and a trained emergency response team will respond to fire and medical emergencies at the site. LNC will perform first response procedures in the event of an emergency. The proposed Project will include a medical facility and an ambulance on site. LNC will construct a helipad by the process plant for medical evacuations. The emergency response team may be available to assist with off-site response if requested by agency personnel or others. However, LNC anticipates that local and regional agencies will maintain sole responsibility for response to incidents outside of the Project area. A separate radio frequency will be established for emergency use, and emergency response and communication protocols will be established.

## 4 Emergency Preparedness

### 4.1 Personal Protective Equipment

Mine personnel, process personnel, contractors, and visitors will be required to wear personal protective equipment, that may include hardhats, steel-toed and steel-shanked boots, leather gloves, eye protection, safety vests, hearing protection and other necessary equipment as required by Mine Safety and Health Administration (MSHA) and/or Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA). Process personnel will also be provided with chemical-resistant gloves, coats, pants, face shields, and dust masks or air-purifying respirators, depending on the task being performed at a given time.

### 4.2 First Aid

First aid kits will be maintained in the mine and process administration buildings, truck shop, laboratory, process building, warehouse, and safety/security building in addition to vehicles and heavy equipment as required by MSHA or OSHA. Personnel will be trained and certified in cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and basic first aid on an annual basis.

<!-- page 7 | printed 3 -->

### 4.3 Fire Extinguishers

Fire extinguishers will be placed in buildings, equipment storage yards, vehicles, and heavy equipment as required by MSHA and OSHA, at a minimum. Fire extinguishers will be of the type required to address the reasonably anticipated class of fire at a given location. Fire extinguishers will be serviced regularly to ensure their proper functioning.

Location and proper use of fire extinguishers will be reviewed with personnel on an annual basis, at a minimum, and upon assignment for new personnel.

### 4.4 Additional Fire Suppression Measures

Fire suppression water systems at the mine facilities and process plant facilities will be installed to provide service throughout the Project site. Fire protection water will be supplied by pump systems specifically designed for fire suppression purposes. Fire water storage will be integrated in the raw water storage tanks with the lower section of the tanks being reserved exclusively for fire water reserves. Fire hydrants will be placed at regular intervals around the buildings and other areas as deemed necessary. The buildings will have sprinkler systems and hand-held fire extinguishers available in accordance with OSHA and MSHA regulations and industry standards. A dedicated fire truck will be located on site for use in structure and equipment fires, as well as wildfires. Personnel will be trained in its operation and will be on call 24 hours per day. Employees will be trained in the use of hand-held fire extinguishers and alarm systems.

LNC will establish a fire protection plan for the Project in accordance with State Fire Marshal standards. Reasonable measures to prevent and suppress fires within the Project area will be taken by employees, contractors, and subcontractors. No open fires will be allowed within the Project area during the mine life.

Smoking will only be permitted in designated areas that are free of flammable materials and only if allowed by state law or federal regulations. If smoking is allowed, smokers will position themselves in such a manner that burning material will fall within cleared areas. Smoking materials will be extinguished by pressing said materials into mineral soils. When completely extinguished, debris associated with smoking will then be put into containers designed solely for this purpose and properly disposed of.

Vehicles and equipment operated on public and private lands and roads will meet proper wildfire preparedness requirements including, but not limited to, being equipped with approved spark

arrestors, fire suppression tools, and other appropriate supplies. Power equipment will be equipped with fire extinguishers, buckets, and shovels during the exploration program.

All mobile equipment will be properly muffled and equipped with suitable and necessary fire suppression equipment, such as fire extinguishers and hand tools. Light vehicles will be fitted with spark arrestors and will carry a small water supply or a fire extinguisher in order to control fires generated by exhaust or catalytic converters.

LNC will coordinate with the BLM to keep vegetation mowed to serve as a fire break at appropriate locations along the fence line at the base of the Montana Mountains. LNC will immediately contact the Central Nevada Interagency Dispatch Center 24-hour hotline and the Orovada Fire Department in the event of a fire and report all wildland fires to BLM and other relevant agencies.

### 4.5 Hazardous Materials Identification

A variety of chemicals and reagents will be used in the mining, processing, and shipping activities at the Project site. Hazardous materials are defined by 49 CFR 172 according to the following characteristics:

- Toxicity;

- Explosive properties;

- Corrosiveness;

- Flammability;

- Oxidizing properties; and,

- Potential for violent or other chemical reaction when mixed.

Safety Data Sheets (SDS) will be maintained in strategic locations for hazardous materials. The SDS provides relevant information on physical characteristics; hazardous reactivity; fire and explosion data; and health hazard information including safety precautions and first aid/medical treatment. Tanks and other containers will be clearly labeled as to contents.

### 4.6 Hazardous Material Spill Prevention and Countermeasures

A variety of hazardous materials will be used at various locations at the mine and process facilities. These materials will be stored at the most efficient location according to their place of use. Small quantities of chemicals will be stored in secure, fire-proof cabinets adjacent to the area of their use. In areas where corrosive materials are stored or used, the concrete will be covered with an impermeable compound, resistant to corrosive chemicals. Only chemical groups compatible with one

<!-- page 9 | printed 5 -->

another may be stored together. Incompatible materials will not be stored in proximity to one another (i.e., same room or cabinet).

Most reagent tanks will be located outdoors within the process facilities in secondary containment. The secondary containment will hold 110 percent of the largest volume tank or tanks in series and, if outdoors and uncovered, additional capacity to hold the 100-year, 24-hour design storm. The floor of the reagent areas will be sealed to prevent spills from entering cracks or permeating the concrete and being released to the environment.

Fuel storage areas with secondary containment will be established at the mine facilities and process plant facilities. Other smaller quantities of hydrocarbons and regulated materials will be located at the truck shop, warehouse, central maintenance, and throughout the process areas. These materials will be kept in their original containers or in containers clearly labeled to indicate their contents. The containers will be stored in storage cabinets or placed within secondary containment.

Spill containment materials, stored at strategic locations throughout the mine, include:

- Oil absorbent rolls;

- Oil absorbent pads;

- Oil absorbent booms;

- Oil absorbent pillows; and,

- Spill kits.

Mobile equipment used for mining operations that will be available for spill cleanup includes:

- Front-end loader;

- Backhoe or excavator;

- Graders; and,

- Dozers.

If the spill is of significant size and/or duration, special cleanup efforts such as those provided by environmental contractors may be deemed necessary.

### 4.7 Fluid Management System

LNC will develop and maintain a Fluid Management and Monitoring Plan which will describe the containment and monitoring of process solutions. This plan will address components associated with process solutions, including the process plant, process ponds, and the Clay Tailings Filter Stack

<!-- page 10 | printed 6 -->

(CTFS) facility. Response and reporting requirements specific to process solutions will be included. This plan will be updated periodically to incorporate improvements identified during implementation.

### 4.8 Communication Systems

The site will be equipped with three radio repeater communication towers. The towers will provide a communication network for the mine and plant sites, as well as GPS correction data. These facilities will support an on-site radio system, communications with outside systems, internet, and cell phones. Wi-Fi will be used on trucks to track hauling and production volumes.

LNC will implement an effective communications network consisting of radios and/or cellular telephones. All vehicles and equipment will be equipped with radios and/or cellular telephones for general operating activities, fire preparedness and prevention, suppression operations, and emergency purposes. All vehicles and equipment will also be equipped with an emergency communication list that will include numbers for the administering agency emergency contact. Additionally, hand-held radios will be available for personnel use.

Internal communications at the site will be by two-way mobile radios. Different channels will be established for mining, process, security, and emergency communications with the mining and process channels designed for repeater antenna capability. A combination of base stations, mobile vehicle radios, and hand-held radios will be available.

In the event of an emergency, “Emergency – Request for Radio Silence” will be announced on all channels of the radio system. When the request for radio silence is announced, all non-emergency radio traffic will cease. If the incident occurs in the pit, all vehicles will pull over and stop until notified otherwise.

External communications regarding emergency situations will be conducted whenever possible by telephone.

### 4.9 Alarm System

A fire alarm system will be installed in the mine and process plant administrative buildings, laboratory, warehouses, truck shops, central maintenance shop, and processing buildings. This system will be used to initiate evacuations and alert personnel of an emergency.

### 4.10 Evacuation Plans [40 CFR §265.52(f)]

If an evacuation is called, the proper general procedures are:

<!-- page 11 | printed 7 -->

- Cease work and proceed directly to designated assembly points/muster sites;

- Obey all evacuation alarms, if applicable;

- Walk - do not run;

- Avoid smoke-filled areas;

- Minimize unnecessary talking or noise;

- Report any missing personnel; and,

- Do not re-enter evacuated areas until the areas are clear and have been instructed to do so.

Evacuation plans will be developed for structures including the administrative building, laboratory, warehouse, truck shop, central maintenance building, and process buildings, as well as other structures that might require evacuation during an emergency. These plans will outline the procedures that should be followed in the event of fire or other emergency requiring evacuation and define responsibilities of key personnel. Evacuation maps that show suggested evacuation routes, muster sites and emergency response routes will be posted at strategic locations throughout the Project site.

### 4.11 Ancillary Power Systems

There will be no ancillary power systems (i.e., diesel generators). Electrical power will be supplied either from the steam turbo generator system in the sulfuric acid plant, or from the Harney Electric transmission line in the event the acid plant is not operating or available. There will be a diesel-powered package boiler in the sulfuric acid plant that will be used to supply emergency steam to areas of the plant in the event that the sulfuric acid plant is not producing steam.

### 4.12 Plant Site Action Plan

An Emergency Action Plan (EAP) is intended to facilitate and organize employer and worker actions during workplace emergencies based on the OSHA standards set forth at 29 CFR 1910 (industry) and 29 CFR 1926 (construction). A comprehensive EAP for workplaces at the LNC plant will be based on hazard assessment to determine what, if any, physical or chemical hazards inside or from outside the workplaces could cause an emergency. The plan will describe how workers will respond to different types of emergencies, considering specific worksite layouts, structural features, and emergency systems.

An EAP will take into the account OSHA standards existing at the time and applicable to the workplace, including 29 CFR 1910 and 29 CFR 1926. Without limiting the effect of the full OSHA standards, the EAP will include:

- Emergency escape procedures and route assignments, such as floor plans, workplace maps, muster sites and safe or refuge areas (example shown in Figure 1) (29 CFR 1910.38(c)(2) and 29 CFR 1926.35(b)(1));

Based on the hazards identified for a particular workplace, and without limiting the full scope of an action plan, other elements of emergency preparedness that may be included in an EAP are:

- Posting emergency numbers in the workplace for the fire brigade, fire department, and other appropriate emergency responders;

- Inviting external emergency responders to tour the facility to learn about hazards, the facility’s processes, protective features and systems, and proper actions to take (or not to take) during emergencies. Tours should account for different shifts of firefighters;

- Coordinating tours for volunteer firefighters at times that accommodate their work schedules;

- Arranging training drills for responders and facility personnel to practice emergency procedures together;

- Designating a facility liaison to coordinate with emergency responders and keep them updated if hazards or processes change;

- Designating one or more emergency contact persons that are knowledgeable of the facility’s hazards and processes and ensure their contact information is quickly accessible during emergencies;

- Designating staff responsible to inventory and maintain emergency equipment and supplies;

- Including a description of the alarm system in the emergency plan to be used to notify workers (including disabled workers) to evacuate and/or take other actions. The alarms used for different actions should be distinctive and might include horn blasts, sirens, or even public address systems;

- Identifying the site of an alternative facility for communications to be used in the event the primary facility is inaccessible because of emergencies, such as a fire or explosion; and

- Storing original or duplicate copies of accounting records, legal documents, worker emergency contact lists, building plans, HAZMAT lists, and other essential records at a secure on-site or off-site location.

The EAP will set forth methods of communication to alert workers, including disabled workers, to evacuate or take other action (see 29 CFR 1910.38(d) and 29 CFR 1926.35(c)). These standards include:

- Employers to ensure that alarms are distinctive and recognized by all workers as a signal to evacuate the work area or perform actions identified in the plan;

- Alarms to be able to be heard, seen, or otherwise perceived by everyone in the workplace. Local fire codes require an auxiliary power supply if electricity is shut off; and,

- The EAP must also state how employees are to report emergencies. Based on the nature of the worksite, the plan may include emergency communication systems such as a public address system, portable radios, or other communications tools to assist in contacting local law enforcement, the fire brigade (if provided), the fire service (e.g., local fire department), and others.

LNC and its emergency response and recovery workers will ensure that plant personnel have and properly use personal protective equipment (PPE) when necessary. PPE can include respiratory protection, splash goggles, hard hat, protective clothing, and protective barriers used to protect workers from exposure to chemical (including oil), biological, radiological and nuclear materials and other hazards. The selection of PPE is based on anticipated hazards and PPE selection may need to be modified as a result of monitoring and assessing actual working conditions. In planning for worker PPE needs, employers should consider the full range of a hazard a worker may experience (e.g., respirator cartridges suitable for both chemical and particulate exposures even if workers may only need protection from particulates most of the time) (29 CFR 1910.132-138).

Different types and levels of PPE may be used depending on the specific hazard or hazards present. PPE also may be needed to protect workers from other hazards, such as electric shock hazards or hazards associated with exposures to hazardous substances that may be encountered during emergency response and recovery operations.

## 5 Emergency Response Procedures

LNC has prepared this ERP pursuant to the requirements of Nevada Administrative Code (NAC) 445A.398(4). This plan describes what actions must be initiated and by whom as a result of releases of pollutants. Emergency response procedures help to organize and prepare personnel for emergency situations by:

- Assisting personnel in responding quickly and effectively to an emergency;

- Providing a common set of practices that govern the activities needed for an orderly response;

- Outlining strategies for early containment and control of an emergency; and,

- Establishing a common set of rules for training all emergency response personnel.

<!-- page 15 | printed 11 -->

### 5.1 Release Response

LNC shall do every practical thing to prevent spills and unauthorized releases. This includes, but is not limited to, regular inspections of control devices, engines, and storage facilities. If coolants or petroleum products are released, they shall be excavated, characterized, and properly disposed of at a facility that is authorized to receive such material, unless otherwise approved in writing by the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection (NDEP). If the release volume exceeds a certain limit, then reporting is required per NAC 445A.345 and NAC 445A.348 and is discussed in Section 5.7.

### 5.3 Roles and Responsibilities [40 CFR §265.52(a)]

EMPLOYEES ARE RESPONSIBLE TO DO THE FOLLOWING IN AN EMERGENCY:

- Notify the area supervisor;

- Report the emergency to the area supervisor and to security;

- Provide help safely until the emergency response team arrives;

- Evacuate the area when instructed to do so;

- Cooperate with the evacuation assembly and counting practices; and,

- Perform shutdown actions according to approved procedures.

SUPERVISORS AND MANAGERS ARE RESPONSIBLE TO DO THE FOLLOWING IN AN EMERGENCY:

- Respond immediately when notified of an emergency;

- Secure the emergency site;

- Ensure safe and orderly evacuation, if called for;

- Assist disabled individuals;

- Conduct head counts at evacuation assembly points/muster sites;

<!-- page 16 | printed 12 -->

- Report any missing personnel to the incident commander;

- Complete shutdown procedures as required; and,

- Supervise personnel at assembly points or during evacuations.

SECURITY PERSONNEL ARE RESPONSIBLE TO DO THE FOLLOWING IN AN EMERGENCY:

- Handle incoming emergency calls and calls to the emergency response team;

- Notify the necessary loss control representatives; and,

- Maintain communications as needed for emergency response.

EMERGENCY COORDINATORS ARE RESPONSIBLE TO DO THE FOLLOWING IN AN EMERGENCY:

- Respond immediately when called upon [40 CFR §265.51(b)];

- Activate necessary alarms and the Emergency Response Team if not already done [40 CFR §265.56(a)];

- Establish control of release site;

- Determine if evacuations are necessary and consider effects of spreading contamination during evacuation procedures;

- Determine if outside assistance is needed; and,

- Determine the possible hazards to human health or the environment by [40 CFR §265.56(c)] soil and/or water contamination.

### 5.4 Emergency Response Actions [40 CFR §265.52(a)]

Procedures to be implemented in the event of a hazardous material release are described in LNC’s Spill Contingency Plan. The Spill Contingency Plan identifies potential sources of spills, establishes measures of prevention, and defines control, cleanup, and reporting procedures, including instructions on what to do in the event of a hazardous material spill.

### 5.6 Spill and Release Reporting Requirements

Spills and releases may include raw material spills, process chemical releases, and chemical fires. Reporting requirements at each facility will follow National Response Center, EPA, NDEP, and any other regulated guidelines when required.

All spills will be reported if the spill involves any amount of a hazardous substance released to surface water; threatens a vulnerable source as defined by NAC 445A.3459; or is a quantity equal to or greater than that which is required to be reported to the National Response Center (40 CFR Part 302).

#### 5.6.1 Incident Reporting Forms

The following is a list of incident reporting forms and checklists that will be developed prior to initiation of operations and made available to personnel:

- Site Safety Plan;

- Checklist for Person Identifying Emergency;

- Emergency Response Team Leader Checklist;

- Incident Scene Checklist;

- Operator Checklist;

- Safety Specialist Checklist;

- Site Access Control Checklist; and,

- Hazardous Materials Checklist.

These forms will be used to document incidents that occur as well as assist mine and plant personnel during an emergency.

### 5.7 Training Plan

Employees will be trained annually in the details of this ERP and that of the Health and Safety Plan prepared for LNC. Training records will be retained in employee personnel files and in the facility operating record. All individuals named in the emergency procedures as described in the ERP will receive proper training to ensure key personnel will know how to react. All personnel must be able to state verbally what their duties are in an emergency.
