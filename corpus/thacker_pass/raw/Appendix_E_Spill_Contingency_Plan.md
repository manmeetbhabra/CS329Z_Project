## 1 Introduction

This Spill Contingency Plan (Plan) for the Thacker Pass Project (Project) is submitted by Lithium Nevada Corporation (LNC) to the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM) and the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection (NDEP). The Thacker Pass Project (Project) is located on public land administered by the BLM. The Project is located in northern Nevada within Humboldt County, approximately 20 miles west-northwest of Orovada, 62 miles north-northwest of Winnemucca, and approximately 20 miles south of the Oregon border. The Project is situated amid Kings River Valley to the west, the Quinn River Valley to the east, the Montana Mountains to the north, and the Double H Mountains to the south in an area known as Thacker Pass.

### 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this Plan is to establish responsibilities and guidelines for actions to be taken by mine and plant personnel in the event of a spill at the Project site. These guidelines are intended to assist personnel and responsible parties in making timely decisions and taking effective actions toward a successful resolution of the problem.

This Plan identifies potential sources of spills, establishes measures of prevention, and defines control, cleanup, and reporting procedures, including instructions on what to do in the event of a hazardous material spill, petroleum release, or natural disaster.

Specifically, the objectives of the Plan are to:

- Reduce the potential for accidental spills and environmental degradation by taking precautionary measures and being prepared for potential emergencies;

- Provide the operating facility with the necessary information to properly respond to a hazardous material emergency situation;

- Define personnel roles for emergencies involving hazardous conditions; and,

- Include a self-audit program to ensure that the Plan and related response activities meet environmental protection objectives.

This Plan is maintained as a stand-alone document assigned to personnel and to individuals on the distribution list provided above. A separate Emergency Response Plan (LNC 2019) was prepared to address emergency response procedures pursuant to Nevada Administrative Code (NAC) 445A.398(A) and release response procedures (NAC 445A.345 and NAC 445A.348). It also serves to describe measures LNC will have in place to avoid spills of chemicals or hazardous substances,

### 2.2 Duties of Mine Personnel

General Manager

The General Manager will be notified as soon as possible when a reportable spill or release occurs. The General Manager will direct all public statements to the media, if required.

Environmental Manager

The Environmental Manager will determine or verify pertinent facts about the incident, including the material spilled or released, amount and location of the spill or release, probable direction and travel time of the spill, resources required at the scene, and the property that may be affected. The Environmental Manager may advise, instruct, and/or direct containment, countermeasures, and cleanup of the materials. The Environmental Manager will assess the area to determine the effect and extent of the spill or release and report the information to the General Manager.

Emergency Response Team

The Emergency Response Team (ERT) includes employees who have been specially trained to work with hazardous materials in a safe and orderly manner. Depending on the type and size of the spill, the ERT may include a Nevada Certified Environmental Manager. The team is trained in the use of all safety gear and will promote and demonstrate safe remediation practices. The prime responsibility of

<!-- page 9 | printed 4 -->

the team is to assess the scene for hazards, secure the area for safety, act professionally, and conduct cleanup procedures as outlined in this Plan.

Safety Officer

The Safety Officer will ensure the safety of all persons involved with a spill or release. Once on the scene, the Safety Officer will evaluate the area for dangers and will ensure that all persons involved are equipped with the appropriate safety gear and have received the proper training. The Safety Officer may also determine if tests for toxic gases are required prior to handling of the spilled material.

Supervisor

The supervisor of an area where a spill or release occurs is responsible for coordinating the initial containment. The supervisor is responsible for determining if the spill will require the ERT. Once the spill or release is controlled, the supervisor must verify if the spill is or is not a reportable spill and notify the Environmental and Safety Departments. At the Project level, supervisors will report to superintendents – one for the mine and one for the process – who are listed in Table 2-1.

## 3 Spill Prevention

### 3.1 Inspections

Tanks, pipelines, and process components will be inspected for leaks and/or damage on a routine basis per industry best practice. Employees, contractors, and other workers on site will be directed to immediately report leaks and damage to the working supervisor for assessment. The working supervisor will be responsible for Plan scheduling and implementing necessary repairs as soon as possible. If discharge has occurred or will occur, the working supervisor is required to inform the Environmental Department, in writing, of the intended schedule and manner of repair. LNC will perform periodic site inspections to ensure proper use and disposal of materials.

<!-- page 11 | printed 6 -->

### 3.2 Transfer of Bulk Liquid and Solid Materials

Employees, contractors, and other workers responsible for transfer of bulk liquid and solid materials will remain at the fill point until fill procedures are completed and the transfer line is placed back in the proper storage location. Spills will be reported to the maintenance supervisor and the Environmental Department, and cleanup will be planned, scheduled, and implemented. LNC’s policy will be to initiate remediation of spills within 24 hours.

### 3.3 Preventative Maintenance

Preventive maintenance will be performed to maintain the mechanical integrity of systems per industry best practice. Cause of release of possible contaminants outside a containment structure (such as faulty valves, joints, elbows, etc.) will be repaired or replaced as soon as practicable, or immediately, if necessary, upon identification. Good housekeeping practices will be implemented at the Project. All materials stored on site will be stored in a neat, orderly manner in their appropriate containers and clearly labelled. LNC will only store the amount of products and materials required for the operation of the Project (to the extent possible). For small containers, products and materials will be kept in their original containers with the original manufacturer’s label.

### 3.4 Spill Containment Structures

Containment structures will be provided for petroleum and liquid reagent storage tanks. Primary and secondary containment structures will be maintained at all locations where failure of a component may result in a potential discharge of process solutions, reagents, process byproducts, petroleum products or other materials. Containment structures will have the capacity to contain at least 110 percent of the largest tank or flanged tanks within the structure, and if out of doors, additional capacity to hold the 100-year, 24-hour design storm. Spill kits will be available in areas where releases could occur.

### 3.5 Emergency Response Procedures

The following is the procedure to be used in the event of a hazardous material release:

1. The first person to identify the release reports the incident to the working supervisor; 2. Supervisor notifies the Environmental Manager;

3. Environmental Manager will be responsible for contacting offsite emergency response teams at the General Manager’s direction;

4. Gather information about the incident;

5. Complete preliminary information on incident report form;

<!-- page 12 | printed 7 -->

6. Contact and transmit information to emergency response team;

7. Emergency response team dispatched to incident;

8. Contact additional emergency units if necessary;

9. Contain spill material and control release;

10. Contact offsite specialists/contractors as required by the circumstances; 11. Remove and secure contaminated material;

12. Arrange for proper disposal of contaminated material;

13. Supervisor completes incident report form;

14. Verbally notify agencies of spill if amount is greater than or equal to the reportable quantity; 15. Follow incident up with a debriefing; and,

16. Evaluate emergency response procedures and modify as necessary.

### 3.6 Emergency Response for Chemical Spills

LNC will receive, store, use, and transport a variety of chemicals at the mine and process facilities. These chemicals will be handled according to industry best practices which will include the use of personal protection equipment, task training, and preventative maintenance. In spite of the training and precautions, unplanned events may occur that require rapid response to protect worker health, prevent or reduce releases to the environment, and reduce damage to equipment.

Many of the chemicals described in this chapter will be used on-site. Personal safety, response, containment, countermeasures, and cleanup procedures for individual chemicals are described in this chapter, although the list is not comprehensive and not all chemicals will always be on site.

#### 3.6.1 Gasoline and Diesel Fuel

Specifications

Gasoline and diesel fuel are shipped to the site by tanker truck in large amounts.

Personal Safety

1. Stay upwind, out of fumes, and keep out of low areas.

2. Wear rubber gloves and boots.

3. No smoking or open flames within 25 feet of gasoline or diesel fuel.

Immediate Response

1. Notify the Environmental and Safety Departments of the spill and request special instructions for personnel safety during cleanup.

<!-- page 13 | printed 8 -->

2. Follow the emergency response procedures as outline in Section 3.5 above. 3. Remove all sources of ignition.

4. Evacuate and isolate the immediate area to avoid personnel exposure.

5. Stop the leak without personal safety risks.

6. Dike the area to contain the spill.

Containment, Countermeasures, and Cleanup

1. Remove all diesel-contaminated soil and place in a designated area for removal and disposal.

2. Gasoline-contaminated soil will be temporarily stored on a synthetic liner and will be covered to prevent volatilization. Contact the Environmental Department for appropriate disposal options.

3. All diesel or gasoline liquids recovered from a spill will be placed in drums or dumpsters for proper disposal.

#### 3.6.6 Quicklime

Specifications

Quicklime (calcium oxide) is shipped by gravity dump trailer and consists of white, odorless solid pebbles or powder that will be conveyed to storage silos. Quicklime is very caustic and can cause chemical burns through prolonged contact with moist skin. Eyes are particularly vulnerable to quicklime releases as loss of site can occur from burns.

Personal Safety

1. Wear an approved dust respirator, work gloves, goggles, and a full covering of clothing. 2. Avoid contact with exposed skin, especially when perspiring.

3. Do not use water near quicklime, as it is very reactive with water.

Immediate Response

1. Notify the Environmental and Safety Departments of the spill and request special instructions for personnel safety during cleanup.

2. Follow the emergency response procedures as outline in Section 3.5 above.

Containment, Countermeasures, and Cleanup

1. Scoop or sweep up spilled lime and place in a suitable container.

2. Excavate the contaminated soil and place within a secured area.

3. The reclaimed lime may be placed into the process circuit with approval from the Process Supervisor.

#### 3.6.8 Molten Sulfur

Specifications

Amber-colored to clear liquid shipped in tanker trucks above the melting point of 235.4 degrees Fahrenheit and used to manufacture sulfuric acid. Although delivered in molten form, transforms into a solid rapidly upon cooling. Solid form of sulfur is pale yellow that is odorless or may have a faint “rotten egg” smell. Burning molten sulfur will emit sulfur dioxide.

Personal Safety

1. Avoid all contact with skin, eyes, or clothing.

2. Avoid breathing dust, or vapors.

3. Avoid combustion by isolating sparks, open flames, and other ignition sources. 4. No smoking.

Immediate Response

1. Evacuate unnecessary personnel. Stop leak if safe to do so.

2. Ventilate area.

3. Notify the Environmental and Safety Departments of the spill and request special instructions for personnel safety during cleanup.

4. Follow the emergency response procedures as outline in Section 3.5 above.

<!-- page 18 | printed 13 -->

Containment, Countermeasures, and Cleanup

1. Contain liquid spills with appropriate barriers and prevent migration and entry into sewers or streams.

2. As an immediate precautionary measure, isolate spill or leak area in all directions. 3. In molten form: allow liquid to solidify before removal.

4. In solid form: Eliminate all ignition sources. Avoid generation of dust during clean-up of spills. Recover the product by vacuuming, shoveling or sweeping. Transfer spilled material to a suitable container for disposal.

5. Excavate contaminated soils and place on a synthetic liner. Contact the Environmental Department for appropriate disposal options.

#### 3.6.16 Aluminum Powder

Aluminum powder is shipped via bulk bag and will be conveyed to the process by a reagent feed system specifically designed for handling aluminum powder. Aluminum powder is flammable and must be kept away from heat, hot surfaces, sparks, open flames and other ignition sources.

## 4 Emergency Services and Contact Information

Depending on the nature of the emergency, mine and plant personnel responding will first contact emergency services via two-way radios installed in vehicles and heavy equipment. Once the immediate threat has been stabilized, the Humboldt County Sheriff’s Office and additional regulatory agencies (as required) will be contacted.

The Project site is not in proximity to any urban areas, so the first responder to any emergency is likely to be trained on-site personnel. The closest dispatch center is provided by Humboldt General Hospital (HGH), located in Winnemucca, approximately 62 miles from the site. HGH is also the

<!-- page 24 | printed 19 -->

closest major medical center to the Project. This facility has an emergency room and other facilities adequate to handle emergencies that may occur. If immediate care is necessary, HGH has a helicopter stationed on site to provide rapid air transportation of critically injured/ill persons. A helicopter will be able to land at the on-site helicopter pad.

Emergency response vehicles and a trained ERT will respond to fire and medical emergencies at the site. The team may be available to assist with off-site response if requested by agency personnel or others. However, LNC anticipates that local and regional agencies will maintain sole responsibility for response to incidents outside of the Project area. A separate radio frequency will be established for emergency use, and emergency response and communication protocols will be established.

## 5 Reporting and Notification

Reportable environmental incidents will be conveyed to the appropriate agencies by LNC within 24 hours of incident stabilization. LNC’s Environmental Manager or designee will be responsible for incident reporting. If the release is determined to be a reportable quantity, the incident will be reported by telephone not later than 5 p.m. of the next regular workday from the time of the incident to:

- NDEP’s 24-hr. emergency notification number at 1.888.331.6337 (in-state) or 1.775.687.9485 (out of state)

- Local Emergency Planning Committee – to be determined

- National Response Center at 1.800.424.8802

- BLM- Humboldt River Field Office at 775.623.3444

- Transportation incidents should be reported to 911

Reporting is required within 24 hours, or the next business day, if the release occurs on a weekend.

If a spill:

- Involves any amount of a hazardous substance released to surface water;

- Threatens a vulnerable source as defined by NAC 445A.3459; or

- Is a quantity equal to or greater than that which is required to be reported to the National Response Center (40 CFR Part 302),
