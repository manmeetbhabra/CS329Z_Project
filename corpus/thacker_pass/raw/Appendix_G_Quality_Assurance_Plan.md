## 1 Introduction

This document is an overview of the Quality Assurance Plan (QAP) for construction of the Lithium Nevada Corporation (LNC) Thacker Pass Project (Project) in Humboldt County, Nevada. This QAP outlines the requirements for quality assurance (QA) plans that will be developed and submitted by Contractors.

Construction of the Project will be undertaken in phases using multiple contractors. A separate design report will be developed for each major Project component and each design report will contain a separate QA plan, specific to the areas of construction. These QA plans will be provided as a guide to field and laboratory personnel conducting inspections and testing for the Project. The QA plans also guide the level of inspection and reporting for Contractors and Construction Managers and will be used in conjunction with the drawings, technical specifications, and relevant and applicable codes and standards.

The following sections outline definitions, roles and responsibilities, and general principles and practice that will be common to each QA plan. The basic premise of the QA plan is to:

- Describe what is to be done;

- Do what is described;

- Describe what has been done;

- Check results;

- Act on the differences; and,

- Document the outcome.

### 2.1 General Project Definitions

Project Construction Drawings and Specifications: Project-related drawings and specifications, including design modifications and record drawings.

Quality Control: Functions performed by the Contractor or supplier to verify that work completed conforms to drawings and specifications.

Quality Assurance: Verification that QC functions have been performed in an adequate and correct manner.

Design Drawings: Approved for construction drawings that show the dimensions, details, and coordinates of the work to be constructed.

Record Drawings: As-built drawings that record the dimensions, details, and coordinates of the work after construction is completed.

### 2.2 Project Organization

Owner: The owner of the Project is LNC.

Contractor: The company contracted to perform fabrication or construction of some or the entire Project.

Construction Manager: Also referred to as the “Project Manager,” the Construction Manager is the official representative of LNC and is responsible for all construction activities including oversight and direction during construction. The Construction Manager is also responsible for coordinating construction and QA activities for the Project. The Construction Manager will serve as a liaison between all parties involved in the Project to ensure that ongoing communications are maintained and will be responsible for the resolution of QA issues that arise during construction. LNC will select the Construction Manager. Duties for this position include the following:

- Review and approve design drawings and Project specifications;

- Coordinate with the Engineer prior to construction to ensure that the design drawings and Project specifications conform to the QA plan;

- Select construction Contractor(s), and administer the construction contracts, including associated changes that occur during the course of construction;

- Coordinate construction activities associated with the various Contractors; and,

- Schedule and coordinate construction activities with required QA testing and activities.

### 2.3 Project Description

The Thacker Pass Project will be an open pit lithium mine and lithium processing operation. The processing facilities are expected to be constructed in two phases (Phase 1 and Phase 2) and produce lithium carbonate, lithium hydroxide monohydrate, lithium sulfide, and lithium metal. Mine operations (including processing) are expected to run 365 days per year, 24-hours per day. Proposed facilities include an open pit mine; mine facilities; attrition scrubbing process; stockpiles (run-of-mine ore and coarse gangue) and waste rock storage facilities (WRSFs); lithium processing plant; sulfuric acid plant; battery production facility; clay tailings filter stack (CTFS) facility; groundwater production wells in Quinn River Valley; stormwater management infrastructure (diversions and ponds); new haul and secondary roads; and, ancillary facilities to support the Project.

### 2.4 Quality Assurance and Quality Control Objectives

The QA/QC objectives for the Project are to ensure that construction is completed safely and in accordance with the applicable drawings, specifications, and standards of workmanship through proper documentation and communication as identified in this QAP. The QC and inspection measures identified will ensure that the specified quality and quantity of work is received from the Contractor and Subcontractors.

As defined in Section 2.1, QC includes functions and procedures that are performed by the Contractor. QA is a process to independently verify and test that the Contractor performs the construction activities to meet the intent of the design and conform to the Project specifications. In general, QA and QC activities will include the following:

- Coordination;

- Documentation;

- Quality control and inspection; and,

- Recording and reporting.

To achieve the QA/QC objectives, Contractors will provide QA plans in the design reports for each major Project component. In addition to the QA plans, LNC will require an acceptable QC Program from the Contractors. The QC Program will be required as part of the submittals procedure, and will be required to be in place prior to selection. In general, the Contractors will be responsible for the QC of construction (e.g., inspections of own work) and the Construction Manager will provide overall QA. The Contractors will also be responsible for QC of mechanical completion and commissioning, and the Construction Manager will be responsible for QA of such.

## 3 Construction and Documentation

To ensure construction is completed safely and in accordance with the applicable drawings, specifications, and standards of workmanship, all personnel at the site must communicate and ensure thorough and accurate documentation of construction activities.

### 3.1 Coordination

It is essential that the Engineer, Construction Manager, QA Monitor, and Contractor coordinate closely to clearly define goals and facilitate construction activities. This will require regular meetings to ensure proper communication. Scheduled meetings will include a pre-construction meeting and weekly progress meetings. Special meetings to address potential problems or deficiencies in either the design or construction should be convened as the need arises.

#### 3.1.1 Pre-Construction Meeting

A pre-construction meeting involving the Engineer, Construction Manager, QA Monitor, and Contractor and others designated by LNC will be held on site prior to construction. Participants will:

- Review construction drawings, specifications, and the QA plan;

- Review work area security, health and safety protocol, and related issues;

- Define the authority and responsibilities of all parties and appropriate lines of communication;

- Review documentation, reporting, and inspection procedures;

- Establish protocols for correcting and documenting construction deficiencies;

- Visit the construction sites to inspect work areas, borrow areas, access and haul roads, and related items; and,

- Review Project schedule.

#### 3.1.2 Progress Meeting

Progress meetings will be scheduled weekly, at a minimum, and will be attended by the Construction Manager, QA Monitor, and Contractor. Possible discussion items include, but are not limited to:

- Health and safety procedures;

- Scheduled work activities; and,

- Potential problem areas, design deficiencies, or opportunities for improvement.

### 3.2 Documentation

Thorough monitoring and documentation of construction activities is required to assure that the construction techniques and procedures are followed by the Contractor and that the intent of the design is met. The results of the QA activities will be documented in the final as-built report by the Engineer.

Daily records will be maintained that consist of field notes, earthwork observations, testing data sheets, summary of meetings with the Contractor, and reporting of construction problems and resolutions. This information will be submitted daily to the Construction Manager for review in a form provided or pre-approved by the Engineer. One complete set of reports will be kept on-site by QA personnel at all times.

## 4 Quality Control and Inspection

The direct purpose of construction inspection is to ensure that the specified quality and quantity of work is received from the Contractor and Subcontractors, i.e., to ensure that the new facilities are constructed in accordance with the applicable drawings, specifications, and standards of workmanship.

QC is the Contractor’s responsibility while QA is the Construction Manager’s and Engineer’s responsibility.

In brief, the QC Representative must enforce the construction-issue drawings and specifications and resolve conflicts where they arise in accordance with the conditions of the contract. In cases of conflicting requirements, the QC Representative must refer to the Engineer’s and Construction Manager’s satisfaction. Most items are covered uniquely and precisely by the specifications as well as in general terms such as “all materials and workmanship will be of first quality and suitable for the purpose intended.” In such cases, it is the QC Representative’s responsibility to see that the Contractor not only has a proper understanding of the requirements but also that the Contractor proceeds accordingly. The QC Representative must ensure compliance with both the minimum specified material and equipment requirements as well as general professional construction standards. Minimum requirements and construction standards will be provided in the QA plans specific to each construction phase.

The QC Representative must be able to visualize conditions, and have a general knowledge of materials, mechanisms, deterioration, processes, and operating problems so that recommendations as to necessary changes and repairs can be made. The QC Representative must possess and use reasonable initiative in taking action on problems encountered and in following generalized instructions. In order to make effective use of their experience, the QC Representative must acquire and maintain familiarity with the Project.

The Contractors will supervise and control those portions of the work that are to be performed under their contracts; will receive, check, unload, place into storage, and warehouse; and control Project materials, machinery, equipment, and supplies. The Construction Manager or Engineer will perform overall supervision and necessary field engineering including the provision of vendor drawings, catalogue cuts, and shop drawings necessary for regulatory review and permitting.

#### 4.4.1 Field Records

Foremost, Field Records must clearly indicate the location of the inspection, type of inspection, and the Inspector’s name. Other findings and observations to report in Field Records include the following:

- Accurate descriptions of what was found during the inspection. These should be clearly written so that others can refer to the data at a later date. The reports should be written as the inspection progresses (daily) and not written from memory at a later date;

- Routine daily reports of construction done completely in compliance as well as reports on items requiring special handling due to discrepancies;

- Special repairs or alterations. These should be described and sketched then shown on the as-built drawings. The method of repair should be clearly described. Future repairs may be influenced by how a repair was made;

- Equipment and materials inspected. These should be completely identified and be signed and dated. Such reports will be kept by the Contractor, with copies to the Engineer and Construction Manager; and,

- Test results. The test results will cite the contract requirements, the test or analytical procedures used, and the actual results, and include a statement that the item tested or analyzed conforms or fails to conform to specification requirements. The Contractor will arrange for immediate and direct delivery of the signed reports, certifications, and other documentation to the Construction Manager and the Engineer.

As the work is completed, the reports should be incorporated into the permanent report folders by the Construction Manager and the Engineer.

### 5.1 Non-Conforming Materials

Any material determined to be out of specification or unacceptable will be placed in a holding area, separate from acceptable material. Conditions for managing non-conforming materials include the following:

- If material is not correct it will be returned except if it is better than specified. Then, if approved by the Engineer and Construction Manager, it may be used. Otherwise it will be returned.

- If the material is out of tolerance or damaged it may be returned or re-worked to meet purchase order requirements.

- If the material is not marked as required by the purchase order, and is otherwise suitable, the vendor personnel may add the required markings, with the exception of pressure vessels.

- Pressure vessel steel missing the fabricators plate will be rejected unless the vendor can document the custody of the steel and provide proof of the vessels fabrication and inspection.

### 5.2 Non-Conformance Work

Unacceptable conditions which can be corrected at the time of discovery and to the satisfaction of the QA Monitor, or which will be corrected during subsequent operations as a matter of due process are not considered non-conformance.

Where work is in progress and continuous testing is being done to bring the work into compliance as long as the final test is in compliance, the in- progress tests do not need to be documented and are not considered non-conformance.

Fabrication non-conformances require the Engineer’s resolution. The Engineer may accept the QC Representative’s recommendation, initiate its own solution, or obtain a solution from LNC. For non-conformance which deviates from LNC requirements, resolution may be any of the following: use as is, reject and return for replacement, or rework to bring into specification. The Engineer will issue drawing instructions required for rework involving a re-design.

### 5.3 Non-Conformance Documentation

Purchase order non-conformance reports will be kept with the purchase order with copies to site purchasing. Installation non-conformance reports will be kept with the contract for installation of the items involved.

The QC Representative will submit a copy of the list on a monthly basis. Items corrected on the day they are discovered do not need to be included on the submitted list.
