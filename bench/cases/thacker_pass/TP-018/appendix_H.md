## 1 Introduction

Lithium Nevada Corporation (LNC) is submitting a Plan of Operations and Reclamation Plan application for the Thacker Pass Project (Project) to the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM). The Project will be located on public land administered by the BLM. Temporary and interim closures are handled by the BLM and Nevada Division of Environmental Protection – Bureau of Mining Regulation and Reclamation (NDEP-BMRR).

## 2 Interim Closure Plan

This Interim Closure Plan fulfills requirements for an interim management plan as defined under 43 CFR 3809.401. The contents of this plan must include, where applicable:

- Measures to stabilize excavations and workings;

- Measures to isolate and control toxic or deleterious materials;

- Provisions for the storage or removal of equipment, supplies, and structures;

- Measures to maintain the Project area in a safe and clean condition;

- Plans for monitoring site conditions during periods of non-operation; and,

- A schedule of anticipated periods of temporary closure during which you would implement the interim management plan, including provisions for notifying BLM of unplanned or extended temporary closures.

Nevada Administrative Code (NAC) 445A.398 states that a temporary closure plan resulting from conditions described in NAC 445A.444 must be prepared. NAC 445A.444 also describes the activities which must be maintained during the time of closure. NAC 445A.444 gives the following examples of planned and unplanned temporary closures which have specific conditions defining their beginning and end:

- Seasonal closures because of normal weather cycles;

- Interruptions in the active beneficiation processes to provide planned periods of quiescence for metallurgical or operating reasons;

- Any other planned process condition which would interrupt the active beneficiation process;

- A closure because of unforeseen weather events;

- A failure in a major system component or a process failure which causes the fluid management system or a portion thereof to shut down; and,

- The discontinuation of a facility’s operations because of litigation.

This Interim Closure Plan covers potential closure scenarios, which are unplanned; therefore, it is structured to be applicable, in general terms, to a range of temporary closure scenarios. This Interim Closure Plan will be updated, if necessary, during the preparation of a water pollution control permit application or permit amendment application. Updates would be submitted to BLM prior to construction of new facilities.

### 2.1 Schedule of Anticipated Periods of Temporary Closure

Mine operations (including processing) are expected to operate 365 days per year, 24 hours per day. No temporary or interim closures of the facility are planned. However, it is possible that mining and process facilities may have to be temporarily closed due to mechanical or technical difficulties, unfavorable economic conditions, litigation, or other unforeseen events. In the event of an unplanned temporary closure, the following plan would be implemented:

- Pursuant to NAC 445.445(1)(a), NDEP would be notified within 30 days of the temporary closure of the process facilities. The BLM would be notified concurrently with the NDEP. This notification would include a description of the procedures and controls that have been or would be initiated to maintain the process components during the temporary closure period.

- LNC would supply the BLM and the NDEP with a list of supervisory personnel who would oversee the mine and plant facility during the temporary closure period. This list also would include the number of support staff required in each department to maintain the facility during the closure period. Standard security procedures would remain in place for the duration of the temporary closure period. Access to the site would be allowed for appropriate regulatory agency personnel.

- Pursuant to NAC 445.445(1)(b)(1), if the interim closure period exceeds 90 days, LNC would begin to evaluate procedures required to carry out a permanent closure of the process components. These procedures would be reviewed and approved by the NDEP and BLM. As stipulated by NAC 445.445(1)(b)(2), LNC may petition the NDEP for an extension that would delay permanent closure. These actions would be coordinated between LNC and the NDEP.

Pursuant to NAC 445A.399, a seasonal closure plan is required for facilities located where the mean diurnal temperature does not exceed freezing (32º F) for 30 days or more each year. Based on a review of available data from the Winnemucca Municipal Airport Meteorological Station data, the mean diurnal temperature at the Project could remain below 32º F for more than 30 days each year. Current plans do not include closure during the winter months; however, if closure is necessary in winter months, it will follow the procedures outlined in Section 3.

### 2.2 Measures to Stabilize Excavations and Workings

No additional measures would be necessary to stabilize excavations and workings during an unplanned temporary closure. Interim reclamation procedures would be implemented as necessary to stabilize disturbed sites during the temporary closure period. These procedures would be coordinated with the BLM and the NDEP.

Pursuant to NAC 445.433(1)(d), adequate storage and conveyance capacity would be maintained in the process facility to accommodate run-off resulting from the 25-year, 24-hour design storm at a minimum.

### 2.3 Measures to Isolate or Control Toxic or Deleterious Materials

Explosive agents may be required on occasion for removing the basalt waste rock material from the pit, but will not be required for operations on a regular basis. LNC will rely on a contractor to conduct blasting operations, if needed. The contractor will ensure all explosives are handled in accordance with the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms, Department of Homeland Security provisions, and Mine Safety and Health Administration (MSHA) regulations. LNC proposes to store the material on the west and east side of the open pit. The explosives storage areas will be fenced and secured to restrict access. In the event of an interim or seasonal closure, explosives would be secured or removed from the Project site.

Hazardous materials would continue to be stored, handled, and disposed of according to federal and state regulations.

### 2.4 Storage or Removal of Equipment, Supplies, and Structures

In the event of a temporary closure, it is anticipated that equipment, supplies, and structures would not be removed or placed into storage. Some mobile equipment or bulk commodities may be relocated into buildings or covered with tarps to remove them from the weather, depending on the anticipated duration of the temporary closure. In addition, the following steps would be taken:

- Additional reagents would not be introduced into process components during the temporary unplanned closure period. Process piping and pumps would be drained if the process circuits are shut down. Stored equipment would be clearly identified as having contained process solutions.

- Mine equipment remaining in operation during the temporary closure, including haul trucks, shovels, loaders, drills, and personal vehicles would continue to be maintained according to standard company procedure.

Following the temporary closure period, the integrity of the entire fluid management system would be evaluated before start-up is initiated. Solution tanks, pumps, and piping would be visually inspected and repaired as necessary. The plant circuit would be charged with process solution and visually inspected for evidence of leaks. Mine equipment would be inspected for compliance with appropriate federal and state mining regulations before mining activities re-commence. Mining activities should not be affected by a temporary closure.

### 2.5 Monitoring During Periods of Non-Operation

All provisions of the water pollution control permit, this Plan, and other regulatory requirements would continue to be met during the temporary closure period. This would include all monitoring, notifications, and report submittals. Site monitoring and monitoring of leak detection systems for vessels and piping containing process solution would continue throughout the temporary closure period.

## 3 Seasonal Closure Plan

This Seasonal Closure Plan has been prepared for the Project pursuant to NAC 445A.399, which requires LNC (the site operator) to prepare a plan for seasonal closure. Specifically, if the facility is in an area where the mean diurnal temperature does not exceed freezing (32º F) for 30 days or more, a plan for the seasonal closure of the process components must be prepared. NAC 445A.399 directs that the Seasonal Closure Plan describes the following:

- The impact this change in the weather will have on the process components, including, but not limited to, a discussion of the possible closure of individual process components;

- Those activities which must be undertaken to prepare those process components which may be potentially affected by the low temperatures;

- The activities which will be maintained during this time of closure; and,

- The conditions that would allow operations to resume.

Mean diurnal temperatures for the Project may not typically remain below 32º F for more than 30 days each year. However, based on meteorological data and information obtained from the Winnemucca Municipal Airport Meteorological Station, extended periods of temperatures below 32º <!-- page 7 | printed 5 --> F (though not necessarily more than 30 days) can occur. As such, this Seasonal Closure Plan has been prepared for process facilities that may potentially be affected by low temperatures.

### 3.1 Process Components Potentially Impacted by Freezing Weather

## Conditions

Process components that transfer water through uninsulated, exposed pipes or pumps are subject to freezing conditions during winter operations. However, process components at the Project site related to processing are designed for operation throughout the winter and do not require seasonal closure planning. Only those process components relating to the storage of water are routinely impacted by freezing weather conditions and require a specific seasonal closure plan.

### 3.2 Seasonal Closure Actions

Although minor equipment failures (i.e., frozen lines or pumps) could briefly affect portions of the operations, the Project is designed so that a total shutdown of process or mining activities due to normal winter conditions is unlikely. However, in the event that an unplanned seasonal closure is necessary due to extremely severe weather conditions, the operation will be temporarily closed in accordance with the measures below:

- The NDEP will be notified within 30 days of the closure per NAC 445A.445(1)(a). The notice will include a description of the procedures and controls that have been or will be initiated to maintain the process components during the seasonal closure period.

- During the seasonal closure period, adequate supervisory and technical support personnel will be available to monitor and maintain the facility.

- Standard security procedures will remain in place for the duration of the seasonal closure period.

- Access to the site will be allowed for appropriate regulatory agency personnel.

- Process piping and pumps will be purged if process circuits are shut down and prepped to prevent freezing and breakage. Stored equipment will be clearly identified as having contained process solutions.

- A small care and maintenance crew would oversee operation, rotate major shafts to prevent bearing damage, cycle certain pieces of process equipment, and maintain a fire watch.

- To ensure that storage is available, any ponds will be monitored periodically, as conditions warrant. If processing circuits have been shut down and it becomes necessary to maintain adequate storage capacity, solutions will be transferred to other ponds or the Clay Tailings Filter Stack using tanker trucks.

- Any mining equipment remaining in operation during the temporary seasonal closure will continue to be maintained according to standard company procedure.

- Explosives will be secured or removed from the Project site.

- Hazardous materials will continue to be stored, handled, and disposed of according to state and federal regulations.

The integrity of the fluid management system will be evaluated before process solutions are reintroduced into the system. The evaluation will consist of visual inspections of the emergency and reclaim ponds liner, process solution tanks, pumps, and piping. Any damaged equipment critical to system integrity will be repaired. The mineral processing circuit will be charged with process solution. A visual inspection of the entire system will again be conducted to check for obvious leaks. If all is in order, standard operating procedures and routine monitoring will ensure the fluid management system's integrity.

Mining activities should not be affected by a seasonal closure. A visual inspection would be conducted to ensure safe conditions before work resumes in the pit area. Mine equipment would be inspected for compliance with appropriate federal and state mining regulations before mining activities re-commence.
