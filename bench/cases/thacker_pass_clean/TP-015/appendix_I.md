## 1 Introduction

Lithium Nevada Corporation (LNC) has prepared this Monitoring Plan (Plan) for the Thacker Pass Project (Project) for submittal to the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM) and the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection, Bureau of Mining Regulation and Reclamation (NDEP/BMRR). The Project is situated at Thacker Pass between the Montana and Double H mountains in Humboldt County, Nevada, approximately 62 miles north-northwest of Winnemucca, Nevada (Figure 1). The Project area is located on public land administered by the BLM.

### 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this Plan is to establish monitoring requirements for the Project during lithium mining and processing operations and reclamation activities. Pursuant to 43 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) 3809.401(b)(4) and Nevada Administrative Code (NAC) 445A.398, a plan for monitoring the effect of the proposed operations must accompany the Plan of Operations (POO). Monitoring plans may incorporate existing state and federal monitoring requirements to avoid duplication. The Monitoring Plan must meet the following objectives (BLM 2009; NDEP/BMRR 2017a):

- Demonstrate compliance with the approved POO and other federal or state environmental laws and regulations;

- Provide early detection of potential problems, and to supply information that will assist in directing corrective actions should they become necessary; and,

- Provide details on type and location of monitoring devices (site-wide monitoring and leak detection monitoring), sampling parameters and frequency, analytical methods, procedures for removal and handling of fluids from leak detection sumps, reporting procedures, and procedures to respond to adverse monitoring results (e.g., quantifying and correcting leaks in liner systems).

As part of the site-wide operating plan, LNC proposes to monitor the following mine components in compliance with state and federal permits and requirements: open mine pit, Waste Rock Storage Facilities (WRSF), coarse gangue stockpile, ore stockpile, processing plant, Clay Tailings Filter Stack (CTFS), reagent and fuel storage, and stormwater management system. The following primary resources will be monitored during operations and reclamation: groundwater, surface water, air quality, wildlife, and noxious and invasive weeds.

This Monitoring Plan has been prepared as an appendix to the POO and Reclamation Plan, but will also be maintained as a stand-alone document for use by onsite personnel in charge of conducting <!-- page 6 | printed 2 --> monitoring activities. The Monitoring Plan accords with the objectives of 43 CFR 309.401(4) to identify and/or prevent impacts to existing resources. Other appendices to the POO that have aspects of monitoring include the Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan and the Noxious and Invasive Weed Management Plan.

This is a preliminary version of the Plan. The Plan will be revised as the Project proceeds and information concerning final permit stipulations and requirements are identified. The Plan will be revised prior to Project commissioning.

### 1.2 Monitoring Requirements

NAC 445A.398(3) requires that LNC, where applicable, prepare a plan for monitoring the Project area which describes:

- Water quality in the area;

- Monitoring locations the applicant proposes to sample routinely in order to evaluate surface and groundwater that may be affected by the operations;

- Analytical profile of each surface water and groundwater monitoring site that may be affected by the operation of the facility; and,

- Locations of leak detection systems, frequency for sampling these systems, and the analytical profile to be used for evaluation of the samples.

This Monitoring Plan describes the methods to be used for monitoring mine and processing-related facilities and operations, and environmental resources, including groundwater, surface water, air resources, wildlife, and noxious weeds. The Monitoring Plan identifies specific points that will be monitored to assess potential impacts to environmental resources by the proposed operation. The types of samples, locations from which samples are collected, parameters to which samples are analyzed, and frequency in which samples are collected, are described in Sections 2.0, 3.0, and 4.0.

## 2 Facility and Operations Monitoring

LNC proposes to construct, operate, reclaim, and close an open pit lithium mining and processing operation – the Thacker Pass Project. The Project has a mine life expectancy of approximately 41 years.

### 2.1 Overview of Facilities

The proposed project developments will occur within the mine and plant area as shown on Figure 2, and will consist of:

- Development of an open pit mine;

- Concurrent backfill of the open pit using waste rock and coarse gangue material;

- Construction of two WRSFs for permanent storage of excavated mine waste rock;

- Construction and operation of mine facilities;

- Construction of a Run-of-Mine (ROM) stockpile;

- Construction and operation of an attrition scrubbing process including an ore slurry pipeline;

- Construction of a coarse gangue stockpile;

- Construction and operation of a lithium processing facility;

- Construction of a sulfuric acid plant and associated energy production;

- Construction and operation of a battery production facility;

- Construction and operation of a Clay Tailings Filter Stack (CTFS);

<!-- page 8 | printed 4 -->

- Construction and maintenance of haul and secondary roads;

- Construction and maintenance of stormwater management infrastructures (diversions and sedimentation ponds);

- Construction of three growth media stockpiles;

- Construction of power transmission lines and distribution;

- Installation of water supply, conveyance pipeline, booster pump stations, and storage infrastructure;

- Construction of ancillary facilities to support the Project such as septic systems, communication towers, guard shacks, reclaim ponds, monitoring wells, weather station, fiber optic line, buffer areas, and fencing.

- The current mine life schedule results in 41 years of commercial mining production with two years of pre-production waste rock removal and stripping concurrent with process facility construction, and an estimated five years of reclamation activities at the end of mine life.

### 2.2 Open Mine Pit

Monitoring the mine pit will be performed in accordance with the Water Pollution Control Permit (WPCP). Common pit wall stability monitoring devices include visual monitoring, prism/laser/radar monitoring, and borehole inclinometers. LNC will review slope stability monitoring results and predictions periodically during the mine life to increase the accuracy of slope stability predictions, and to adjust pit designs based on actual mining experience.

Groundwater monitoring wells have been installed in the vicinity of the mine pit to periodically assess water quality and water levels during mine development. These wells are installed and monitored following the NDEP/BMRR (2018) guidance “Guidance Document for the Design and Construction of Groundwater Monitoring Wells and Approved Monitoring and Sampling Methods”. The final list of monitoring wells, frequency of monitoring, and analytical parameters will be specified and approved by NDEP/BMRR in the WPCP. Also see Section 3.1.1 for further descriptions of the groundwater monitoring system.

### 2.3 Waste Rock Storage Facility, Coarse Gangue, and Run-of-Mine Ore

## Stockpiles

Monitoring the WRSFs, coarse gangue stockpile, and ROM stockpile will be performed in accordance with the WPCP and Waste Rock Management Plan. The Waste Rock Management Plan, which will be finalized once all rock testing is completed, will describe how the material is identified by testing prior to and during mining, selectively handled, processed, and reclaimed.

<!-- page 9 | printed 5 -->

Quarterly samples of representative waste rock, coarse gangue, and ore material will typically be collected during the mine life. Sampling and analysis will be conducted in accordance with NDEP/BMRR (2019) guidance “Waste Rock, Overburden, and Ore Characterization and Evaluation” (22 March 2019), and Nevada BLM (2013) guidance “Rock Characterization and Water Resources Analysis Guidance for Mining Activities” (Instruction Memorandum No. NV-2013-046). Meteoric Water Mobility Procedure (MWMP) samples will be analyzed for the NDEP Profile I list of parameters (see Section 3.1) or as otherwise stipulated by the WPCP.

Groundwater monitoring wells have been installed in the vicinity of the various stockpiles to assess water quality and water levels. The final list of monitoring wells, frequency of monitoring, and analytical parameters will be specified and approved by NDEP/BMRR in the WPCP. NDEP/BMRR guidance for monitoring wells will be followed as described in Section 2.2 above, and Section 3.1.1 below.

### 2.4 Processing Plant Facilities

Monitoring the processing plant facilities, including sulfuric acid plant, will be performed in accordance with the WPCP and air quality permit. Leak detection systems for the processing systems will be checked periodically and sampled if fluids are present. Emission sources from the processing plant facilities, including sulfuric acid plant, will require monitoring (see Section 3.2).

The NDEP/BMRR (2017b) guidance “Permit Limitations for Leak Detection Systems” will be followed for allowable flow rates in gallons per day. In the event that permit limitations are exceeded, a site-specific evaluation will be conducted by LNC to assess the need for additional process component or site monitoring. Depending on site-specific conditions, LNC may be required to institute mitigation procedures to bring the process component into compliance.

Groundwater monitoring wells have been installed in the vicinity of the processing plant facilities and will be used to supplement the leak detection monitoring. The final list of monitoring wells, frequency of monitoring, and analytical parameters will be specified and approved by NDEP/BMRR in the WPCP. NDEP/BMRR guidance for monitoring wells will be followed as described in Section 2.2 above, and Section 3.1.1 below.

### 2.5 Clay Tailings Filter Stack

Monitoring the CTFS will be performed in accordance with the WPCP. Drainage from the CTFS will be directed to the reclaim ponds. <!-- page 10 | printed 6 -->

Tailings draindown and solids chemistry will be monitored as part of the process facility area chemistry monitoring. Draindown samples are typically collected quarterly and analyzed for either NDEP Profile I or Profile II parameters. Quantities of tailing solids also will be recorded quarterly. Drainage from the CTFS will be checked periodically and sampled if fluids are present. NDEP/BMRR guidance for leak detection systems will be followed as described in Section 2.4.

Groundwater monitoring wells have been and will be installed in the vicinity of the CTFS to periodically assess water quality and water levels. The final list of monitoring wells, frequency of monitoring, and analytical parameters will be specified and approved by NDEP/BMRR in the WPCP. NDEP/BMRR guidance for monitoring wells will be followed as described in Section 2.2 above, and Section 3.1.1 below.

### 2.6 Reagents and Fuel Storage

Monitoring the reagent and fuel storage areas will be conducted in accordance with the WPCP. This monitoring will typically entail periodic visual inspections of the storage facilities and inventories of reagent and fuel quantities onsite. The final list of monitoring wells, frequency of monitoring, and analytical parameters will be specified and approved by NDEP/BMRR in the WPCP. NDEP/BMRR guidance for monitoring wells will be followed as described in Section 2.2 above, and Section 3.1.1 below.

### 2.7 Water Management System

A Fluid Management and Monitoring Plan will be prepared as part of the WPCP application and submitted to BLM and NDEP prior to construction. Chemical storage, dispensing, and processing areas will be monitored periodically for signs of spillage or potential equipment failure. LNC will monitor disturbed areas for signs of erosion, sediment accumulation, and potential offsite discharges. Inspections of sediment controls will include the following activities:

- Inspection of material handling areas for evidence of, or potential for, pollutants entering the drainage and conveyance system (non-structural controls); and,

- Inspection of erosion control systems and sediment control devices (structural controls) in areas of material handling and along transportation corridors to verify they are working effectively and/or to determine if maintenance is required.

As mentioned above in Sections 2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, and 2.6, groundwater monitoring wells have been installed in the vicinity of water management systems to periodically assess water quality. The final list of monitoring wells, frequency of monitoring, and analytical parameters will be specified and approved by NDEP/BMRR in the WPCP. NDEP/BMRR guidance for monitoring wells will be followed as described in Section 2.3 above, and Section 3.1.1 below.

### 2.8 Sediment Controls

LNC will monitor disturbed areas for signs of erosion, sediment accumulation and potential offsite discharges. Another appendix to the POO comprises the Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan that includes BMPs for erosion and sediment controls. Inspections of sediment controls would include the activities outlined below.

- Inspection of material handling areas for evidence of, or the potential for, pollutants entering the drainage and conveyance system (non-structural controls); and,

- Inspection of erosion control systems and sediment control devices (structural controls) in areas of material handling and along transportation corridors to verify that they are working effectively and/or to determine if maintenance is required.

## 3 Environmental Resources Monitoring

### 3.1 Water Resources

LNC will operate the Project as a zero-discharge facility. Specific monitoring measures will be defined during other federal and state groundwater and surface water permitting processes (e.g., WPCP), which will ensure compliance with the Clean Water Act (CWA) and the Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA).

The Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP; in an appendix of the POO) identifies more specific control measures to reduce or mitigate potential impacts, and monitoring requirements to ensure that the best management practices (BMPs) implemented at the site are functioning as designed during the construction, operation, reclamation phases of the Project.

## 1 Introduction

The Thacker Pass Project (Project) is a proposed open pit mine and lithium processing operation to be owned and operated by Lithium Nevada Corporation (LNC) that will use conventional mining and proprietary processing technology to extract clay and lithium with a life expectancy of approximately 41 years. LNC prepared this Quality Assurance Project Plan (QAPP) as part of the Project’s overall Monitoring Plan. The Project is situated at Thacker Pass between the Montana and Double H mountains in Humboldt County, Nevada, approximately 62 miles north-northwest of Winnemucca, Nevada. The Project site is located primarily on public land administered by the BLM.

As part of the site-wide operating plan, LNC proposes to monitor the following mine components in compliance with state and federal permits and requirements: open mine pit; Waste Rock Storage Facilities (WRSF); ore stockpiles; processing plant and associated ponds; Clay Tailings Filter Stack (CTFS); reagent and fuel storage; and water management system.

This QAPP describes common quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC) methods that can be used for monitoring data associated with mine-related facilities and operations, and environmental resources (e.g., groundwater, surface water, and air resources). The QAPP is intended to provide consistency in the protocols and procedures related to Project monitoring and should be used in conjunction with the Monitoring Plan.

The QAPP outlines quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC) procedures that are typically followed to document that monitoring data are representative for the sample and measurement locations. Monitoring data are usually validated, where possible, to identify any unreliable or invalid field and laboratory measurements, and qualify data for interpretive use. Data that do not meet acceptance criteria will be noted or flagged in the final reported database/tables.

## 3 Data Review, Verification, and Validation

Field data from each monitoring event should be verified and validated. Data reduction of field measurements will be performed. The laboratory will calculate QC parameters for its QC samples. #### 3.1.1 Data Review

The ability of data to meet Project requirements will be summarized in a precision, accuracy, representativeness, completeness, and comparability (PARCC) statement. A PARCC statement is generated during data evaluation and validation. The following sections define terms used in the five-part narrative PARCC statement.

#### 3.1.2 Precision

Precision is a measure of the scatter or variance that occurs in repeated laboratory measurements. Precision acceptance and rejection for this project will be based on the relative percent difference (RPD) of the field and laboratory duplicates, or absolute value difference as described below. When both values of the natural/duplicate pair are greater than five times the PQL for a given analyte, analytical results for the duplicate samples will be evaluated by the RPD comparison. #### 3.1.3 Accuracy

Accuracy is measured as the ability to detect a known quantity of a particular substance in a sample. Accuracy acceptance or rejection will be based on the percent recovery of the Matrix Spike (MS) and the Laboratory Control Sample (LCS). Additional sample volume may be collected to allow laboratory MS evaluations of project samples. To determine accuracy, the percent recovery for each LCS will be compared to the acceptable ranges established by the laboratory. Results associated with LCS percent recoveries outside acceptable limits will be considered estimated and qualified with “J”. Field sample results associated with MS percent recoveries outside of laboratory-established control limits will be considered estimated or rejected, as appropriate in accordance with EPA MS criteria (EPA 2010). Estimated results will be qualified with “J”.

An additional accuracy check is comparison of dissolved versus total fractions for metals results where both fractions are analyzed by the laboratory. In order to be accurate, the total fraction must be greater than or equal to the dissolved fraction, within the precision of the analysis. Such total/dissolved pairs will be evaluated for relative magnitude and RPD (20%) or PQL Comparison, as appropriate, with any results flagged using the qualifier “J” for results with higher dissolved fractions than total fractions.

#### 3.1.4 Representativeness

The objective in addressing representativeness is to assess whether measurements obtained during monitoring reflect site conditions in the sampling unit or target area. Representativeness is evaluated by review of sampling design to confirm that areas reasonably suspected to have different characteristics were sampled separately. In addition, blank QC samples (laboratory blanks and/or field blanks) are used to evaluate representativeness.

If a laboratory blank contains detectable levels of common laboratory contaminants, then natural sample results will be considered as positive only if the concentrations in the natural sample exceed 10 times the maximum amount detected in any blank. If all samples contain levels of a common laboratory contaminant at less than 10 times the contamination noted in the blank, then the analyte will be eliminated from the set of sample results. For other analytes that are not common laboratory contaminants, the following criteria will be used when detections in the blank are greater than or equal to the PQL:

- If the concentration in the natural sample is less than 10 times the blank concentration, but greater than or equal to the blank concentration, the associated natural samples will be flagged with “J”.

<!-- page 36 | printed 9 -->

- If the natural sample concentration is less than the blank, the natural sample result will be changed to non-detect, with a reporting limit equal to the concentration in the blank, and qualified as “J”.

#### 3.1.5 Completeness

The objective in addressing completeness is to assess whether enough data have been collected and enough data are valid to meet the assessment needs. Valid data include both qualified/estimated data and unqualified data, but does not include rejected data. Completeness is evaluated by comparing the number of valid sample results to the number of samples collected, including required QC samples. A completeness target goal for the Project is 90 percent.

#### 3.1.6 Comparability

The objective in addressing comparability is to assess whether one set of data can be compared to another set of data. Comparability is assessed by determining if an EPA-approved analytical method was used, if values and units are either identical or mutually convertible, if specific sampling points can be established and documented, if field collection methods were standardized and documented, and if QC results are provided in-full for the dataset. If data meet all other comparability requirements, but have limited QC results not fully meeting the standards, a statement will be provided regarding the comparability of the dataset to those meeting all requirements of the plan.

In order to be comparable between datasets and to be useable for decision-making purposes, the laboratory reporting limits used will be below the screening levels or compliance thresholds for each medium.

#### 3.1.7 Data Validation

The objective of data validation is to identify any unreliable or invalid measurements and qualify the data for interpretive use. Data validation will be performed according to EPA (2010) guidelines. This involves determining if laboratory and field documentation is provided in-full, and if all QC results are available. As part of data validation, the set of field QC samples will be reviewed against required frequencies; QC metrics will be evaluated against specified thresholds, and data qualifiers will be added.

Knowing limitations of the data assists the user when interpreting the data. Data with limitations are usable for evaluation as long as the limitations are considered. Professional judgment is required and will be used to assess the impact of data concerns on the overall quality and usability of the field and laboratory data.

#### 3.1.8 Data Reconciliation

Data reconciliation is performed after data validation is complete. Data reconciliation is generation of the PARCC statement that describes whether the data met the Project requirements. Data that do not meet Project requirements may require corrective measures depending on the ultimate use of the data. Corrective measures may be implemented in the following sequence:

1. Samples will be re-analyzed

2. Samples will be re-collected for analysis

3. Data will be rejected

In the event that the laboratory notifies monitoring personnel of missing, broken, or lost samples, the following corrective measures may be taken: (1) re-collection of missing or broken samples, if possible; or (2) elimination of the data.
