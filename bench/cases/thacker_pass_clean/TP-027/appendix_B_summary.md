# Appendix B – Waste Rock and Ore Characterization Report (summary)

**Full title:** Preliminary Baseline Geochemical Characterization Report for the Thacker Pass
Project – Draft Report. Prepared for Lithium Nevada Corporation by SRK Consulting (U.S.), Inc.,
Reno, Nevada (SRK project 357800.180). Dated August 1, 2019, revised October 11, 2019.
1,483 pages, including laboratory data appendices.

**Note:** The full report was submitted as Appendix B to the Plan of Operations. It is
summarized here and not reproduced in full. This summary is limited to the report's scope
and its own summary of results (Section 6, printed pages 100–102).

## Scope

- **Purpose:** Characterize the potential for acid rock drainage and metal leaching (ARDML)
  from the waste rock, ore, gangue and tailings of the proposed Project, to support permitting
  under 43 CFR 3809.401(b)(2) and (c)(1), BLM Instruction Memorandum NV-2013-046, and NDEP Water
  Pollution Control Permit guidance (report Sections 1–3).
- **Materials:** core samples of alluvium, basalt, ash, claystone/ash, claystone, the hot-spring
  (HPZ) unit and Tertiary volcanics (Tv). Process samples of feed ore, coarse gangue, clay
  tailings, neutralization solids and sulfate salts from LNC's research and development
  facility (report Section 4).
- **Tests:** multi-element analysis, acid-base accounting (ABA), net acid generation (NAG),
  meteoric water mobility procedure (MWMP), radiochemistry, and kinetic humidity cell tests
  (HCT). The sample counts by material type are given in Table 3-4 of the Plan.

## Key results (report Section 6)

**Waste rock and ore (p. 100–101)**

- Net neutralizing, with an average neutralization potential ratio (NPR) greater than 3 for all
  material types. The kinetic testing confirmed the low acid-generation potential.
- About 2% of the samples (ash, claystone, claystone/ash) are predicted to be potentially acid
  generating (PAG). In the block model, using an NPR cut-off of 1.2, PAG is estimated at 0.25% of
  total waste rock and about 1% of the final pit wall surface.
- Segregated waste rock management "does not appear to be necessary and is not recommended".
- Antimony and arsenic are consistently released above NDEP Profile I reference values in the
  kinetic tests. Fluoride, iron, magnesium, manganese and sulfate are flushed above Profile I in
  weeks 0–4, then equilibrate lower. Uranium is initially flushed above Profile I-R, then drops
  below it within weeks. Arsenic is naturally elevated in local groundwater.
- Kinetic testing of one alluvium sample and one ash sample is ongoing, for a minimum of 20 weeks
  or until BLM and NDEP approve termination.
- Numerical predictions of solute concentrations from the backfilled pit were prepared by Piteau
  under separate cover.

**Gangue (p. 101)**

- Gangue is planned for use as pit backfill, together with waste rock, to prevent a permanent
  post-closure pit lake. It will not be used as backfill until about year 13 of mining.
- Ore feed is net neutralizing (average NPR 17), and gangue is net neutralizing (average NPR 24).
  Both can leach arsenic and antimony under neutral to alkaline conditions.
- The wet attrition process increases aluminum, arsenic, antimony, iron and manganese in MWMP
  leachate. Radionuclides leach from gangue and ore feed below Profile I-R.
- Results for twelve unoxidized gangue samples are pending. Characterization of gangue will
  continue as mining goes deeper.

**Tailings (p. 102)**

- Clay tailings contain little sulfide but carry residual sulfuric acid from processing, which
  produced low pH in the ABA and NAG tests. Many metals (including aluminum, arsenic, antimony,
  beryllium, cadmium, chromium, copper, fluoride, iron, lead, manganese, mercury, nickel,
  thallium and zinc), sulfate and TDS leached above Profile I under low-pH conditions.
- For the clay tailings sample, uranium, gross alpha and radium-226/228 exceed Profile I-R.
  Neutralization solids and sulfate salts produce circum-neutral to alkaline leachate with lower
  concentrations.
- Because of this leaching potential, the tailings facility will be a **zero-discharge
  facility**, covered with waste rock and growth media at closure.

**Next steps (p. 100–102)**

The report states that geochemical testing is ongoing. An updated Characterization Report will
give final results and final recommendations for managing waste rock, gangue and tailings.
